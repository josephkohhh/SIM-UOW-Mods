Lecture 4: Outward-oriented Growth

It is an economic development strategy where a country promotes exports 
and integrates with the global economy instead of protecting domestic industries.
- It is closely associated with the Neoclassical approach
- Opposite from Structuralist Approach


Main Idea of Outward-oriented Growth
- Produce goods for the world market
- Encourage international trade
- Compete globally
- Promote efficiency through competition


Basis of Trade – Heckscher-Ohlin (HO) Theory
Countries export goods that use their abundant production factors intensively 
and import goods that use their scarce factors


Factor Intensity
How much of each production factor is required to produce a product

Ex. 
T-shirt requires (1 skilled worker, 2 unskilled workers) = Unskilled labour-intensive
Computer Chips requires (3 skilled worker, 3 unskilled workers) = Skilled labour-intensive

Formula is Ls divide by Lu (Ls/Lu) 
- above Ex is computer chips more skilled intensive than tshirt (1 > 0.5)


Factor Endowment
Refers to a country's available resources

Ex. 
- Country A has more skilled workers = produce computer chips
- Country B has more unskilled workers = produce t shirts
- Hence Country A will import tshirt and export chips to country B 
- Hence Country B will import chips and export tshirt to country A


But what if countries are new players in the international market who needs to compete?


Role of Government
Govt help firms become internationally competitive through
- Increase Export earnings

export earnings = Price × Quantity

Govt can increase:
- selling price (charge higher price thru better quality&branding)
- export quantity (increase qty sold)

Currency Devaluation 
A deliberate reduction in the value of the domestic currency by the central bank
- make Peso becomes weaker

Ex.
Before:
1 USD = 100 Peso

After:
1 USD = 200 Peso


Foreign buyers need fewer USD to buy Colombian goods
- Exports become cheaper
- Exports increase

Currency devaluation leads to 
- increase in exchange rate (E)
- decrease in foreign price of exports
- higher export demand 
- higher export


Problems with Currency Devaluation

Inflation
- imports become more expensive
  - Imported US beef costs more after the peso weakens

Currency War
- Other countries may also weaken their currencies
- This creates:
  - retaliation
  - competitive devaluation
  - instability
