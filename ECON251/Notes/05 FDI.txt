Lecture 5: Foreign Direct investment (FDI)

Recap:
Capital Goods (K) described as tools and machinery used in production of goods and svc
- Spending on capital goods = fixed capital formation

K is expensive, thus requires funding and involves risk 

Foreign Direct Investment
- a company buying or building a factory in another country

Foreign Portfolio Investment
- buying stocks and bonds from another country 

 
Types of FDI
- Greenfield (a foreign company starts a completely new biz/facility in another country) = build
- Brownfield (a foreign company buys an existing biz/facility in another country and uses it for its biz) = buy
- Joint Venture (a foreign company partners with a local company or government to contribute money, tech, resources and share biz) = partner


Reasons for FDI
1. Gain closer proximity (gain closer proximity to a market)
2. Evade tariffs (when other country imports ur goods)
3. Reduce tax burden (taking advantages of lower tax rates)

Possible reasons for firms to operate overseas:
- Expand revenue Growth = Higher Revenues (Demand-Side Factors)
- Large Domestic Host Market = Access new domestic consumer base 
- Proximity to Consumers = Reduce lead time by being physically closer to consumers
- Political and Economic Stability = Escape from unstable economy and political environment
- Openness to Trade and Investment = Low tariff barriers, favorable foreign ownership limits
- Tax Incentives and Subsidies = Developing nations offer corporate tax holidays, special economic zone (SEZ) benefits, 
                                 or custom duty exemptions to lure high-value industries like semiconductor


FDI and effects on hosting countries  
- Labour Market (employment, wages, labour productivity)
- Economic Growth (Growth in manufacturing sector)
- Spillovers (R&D, Networks, Knowledge, Reputation)


Possible harms of FDI 
- Exploitation of resources, Threat to environment  
- Threats to local smaller firms competition for resources and market 
- No loyalty and thus can create a hollow in the economy 



Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)
- buying stocks and bonds from another country 

Harrod Domar Model 
g = s/v

g = economic growth rate 
s = national savings rate = level of funding 
v = capital - output ratio = k/y 

- A higher g can be generated by a higher level of savings rate (s) 
- A higher g can be generated by a lower level of v (where Y rise faster than K)

As foreign money buy up assets in stocks and bonds
- banks have more money to lend 
- this encourages money lending 
- creates asset inflation 
- debt rises    

Capital controls and market monitoring to prevent money laundering
Reserve accumulation to prevent sudden capital flight and currency volatility 


Argument against FDI inflows
- The MNCs sought the host country only as a tax haven,  and not generating enough income and employment to the host country
- The MNCs truncate the production process, minimising the level of technology and knowledge transfer in the host country 
- A phenomenon of resource distortion where funds, labour are steered towards the MNCS and away from the local firms. 

- To solve this, promote join venture between local firms and the MNCs
