Asia's Economies
- East Asia (China, Japan, South Korea, etc.) — defined by shared language and culture
- Central Asia (Afghan, Uzbekistan, etc.) — mountainous terrain
- South Asia (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, etc.) — agriculture-based economies
- Southeast Asia (Singapore, Indonesia, Vietnam, etc.) — Multiracial, strong growth and development focus
- West Asia (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iran, etc.) — resource-rich economies: oil


Theoretical / Economic Analysis Tools

GDP & Output
Real GDP (RGDP) = total value of goods and services, adjusted for inflation

3 ways to measure RGDP
- Income (add up everyone's earnings)
- Spending (add up everything bought)
- Output - value added (add up what each industry contributes)

Spending method formulas:
- Closed economy (no trade): GDP = C + I + G
- Open economy (with trade): GDP = C + I + G + X − M

C = household consumption
I = business investment
G = government spending
X = exports
M = imports

Value Added avoids counting the same thing twice. 
For ex, flour costs $2 and bread sells for $8, only the $6 diff counts as new value added by the baker


Inputs of Production — "What goes into making things?"
These are the building blocks firms use to produce goods:
- Labour (L) — workers
- Capital (K) — machines, buildings, equipment
- Others — raw materials, technology, human capital (skills/education)

Demand & Supply — "How are prices set in a market?"
- Demand (Dd) = what consumers want to buy
- Supply (Ss) = what producers want to sell
- Equilibrium = where they meet (price P*, quantity Q*) — the "happy" market price

The key thing to understand is what shifts the curves:
- If oil supply drops due to war → supply curve shifts left → prices rise
- If everyone wants to travel post-COVID → demand curve shifts right → airfares rise


Trade & Exchange Rates — "How does currency affect trade?"
- SGD 1 = USD 0.77 (i.e. 1 Singapore dollar buys 0.77 US dollars)

What happens when SGD appreciates (gets stronger)?
- Imports go up (SGD can buy more USD → foreign goods are cheaper)
- Exports go down (Foreigners need more USD to buy SG goods → they buy less)

Simple rule: Stronger currency = cheaper imports, more expensive exports
