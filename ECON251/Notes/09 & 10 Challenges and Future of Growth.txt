L09 & L10: Challenges and Future of Growth

1. Income Inequality

GDP per capita hides inequality — averages can be misleading

Ex.
Island of 10 people, total GDP = $1,000 → GDP per capita = $100

Reality: 
20% of population (2 people) own 60% of income ($600) → $300 each
Remaining 8 people share $400 → ~$50 or less each


Measurement: Gini Coefficient
- Widely used measure of income inequality
- Range: 0 (perfect equality) to 100 (perfect inequality)
- Higher Gini = greater inequality

Common Myths About Inequality
- Occurs mainly in large, populous countries (Indonesia, India, China)
- Occurs where there's a significant urban–rural divide
- Seen as a "rich countries' problem"
- Seen as "inevitable"

Why It Occurs / Why Hard to Measure
As infrastructure and education improve, governments collect more taxes to help displaced populations

Open question: does tackling inequality slow down trade and industrialisation?


Why It Matters
- Growth-focused economies often ignore poverty/inequality, expecting income to "trickle down"
- Policy focus: move more people into middle-income status
- The poor and middle class can be a driving force for growth through interrelated economic, 
  social, and political channels — especially important for populous Asian economies


2. Aging Population

Dependency Ratio = Non-working age population : Working age population

Aging population + declining birth rates → dependency ratio rises

Economic Implications of Aging Population / Rising Dependency Ratio
- Labour shortages
- Younger generation pays higher taxes
- People work longer hours
- Greater government spending on healthcare and infrastructure for the elderly
- Lower tax collection (fewer working-age taxpayers)

Ways to Lower the Dependency Ratio
- Increase retirement age
- Increase labour force participation (e.g., ASEAN regional collaboration)
- Use of AI/automation to offset labour shortages


3. Energy, Environment & Climate Change

Asia is a major contributor to global CO₂ emissions in absolute terms
- China
- India
- Japan

Climate Change Risks
- Floods, storms, forest fires, droughts
- Costs are increasing, particularly for South, East, and Southeast Asia
- Risk of GDP loss and potential financial crisis

Economic Costs of Climate Change
- Food Security
  - Agriculture is the most vulnerable sector to climate risk
  - Disruption to crop yields and crop quality

- Infrastructure
  - Flooding threatens/damages infrastructure (Loss of asset value)
  - Disruption to communication systems (e.g., optic fiber networks)
  - Affects flows of FDI (Foreign Direct Investment)

- Market Distortions
  - Production and supply chain disruptions increase operational costs
  - Government policies (e.g., carbon tax) distort prices to mitigate climate change
  - Government resources rechanneled toward climate mitigation

Regional Responses
- Asian collaboration: cross-border flows of renewable energy (e.g., ASEAN Power Grid — cross-border HVDC links like Thailand–Malaysia)
- Key regional institutions: Asian Development Bank (ADB), Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)


4. The Future of Asia: Collaborate or Compete

Changing Trade Patterns
- "China +1" Strategy
  - Corporations diversify away from single-source manufacturing in China
  - Redirect investment to ASEAN (Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia) to mitigate geopolitical risk

- RCEP Integration (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership)
  - Streamlines rules of origin across 15 nations
  - Cuts transaction friction, anchors pan-Asian supply chains

- Decentralized Value Networks
  - Shift from linear final-assembly lines to complex regional value chains (RVCs)
  - Scales intermediate goods exchange across Southeast Asia


The Geopolitical Race for Minerals and Energy

1. Critical Minerals Hub
- ASEAN economies (esp. Indonesia — nickel) hold raw materials vital for EV batteries and high-tech electronics

2. Energy Security
- Industrialization + AI-driven data centers require massive power scaling
- Nations must secure reliable, green, and transitional energy grids

3. Resource Nationalism
- Export bans on raw ores + industrial policy push for domestic refining
- Goal: convert mineral wealth into higher-value domestic manufacturing leverage
