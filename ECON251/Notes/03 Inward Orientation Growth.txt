- Understand the role of:
  - Trade protection
  - Currency overvaluation
  - Government assistance

1. Background
Developing Countries' Problem

Most developing countries relied heavily on the primary sector.

Ex:
- Agriculture
- Mining
- Raw materials

Characteristics:
- Low GDP
- Slow economic growth
- Unstable export earnings
- Dependence on world commodity prices
- Low productivity

Meanwhile, developed countries focused on manufacturing.

Manufacturing provides:
- Higher value-added products
- Stable income
- More employment
- Better technology
- Stronger economic growth

Key Question:
Should governments continue developing agriculture or build a manufacturing sector?


2. Problems When Building Manufacturing
Manufacturing requires large investments (K)
- Factories
- Machinery
- Equipment

Countries often lacked:
- Infrastructure
  - roads, electricity, water supply, ports, communications
- Knowledge
  - few skilled workers, little industrial experience, weak technology
- Labour
  - workers need formal training before joining manufacturing industries

Market Problem
Even if local factories produced goods:
- Would consumers still prefer imported products?


Structuralist Approach
- Government should actively transform the economy from agriculture into manufacturing

Unlike free markets, the government would decide:
- Which industries to develop
- How much to protect industries
- What policies to introduce

Government intervention is therefore very high

2 Development Strategies:

1. Inward Orientation
- Trade protection
- Focus on domestic market
- High government intervention


2. Outward Orientation
- will discuss in lecture 4


Inward-Oriented Growth aka Import Substitution Industrialisation (ISI)
- Instead of importing manufactured goods, produce them domestically.
- The goal is to reduce dependence on imports.


Main Policies
Government uses:
- Trade protection
- Overvalued currency
- Government assistance
- Restrictions on imports


Infant Industry Problem
A new manufacturing industry that:
- Is small
- Is inexperienced
- Cannot compete internationally

The industry needs time to grow

Why Protect It?
Protection allows firms to:
- Learn production techniques
- Improve efficiency
- Gain experience
- Eventually become internationally competitive

Without protection,
- foreign firms may drive local firms out of business


Trade Protection
Trade protection limits imports, allow domestic firms to grow
- Tariffs
- Quotas


Trade protection 
Pros:
- Supports Local Industries, Encourages Manufacturing, Employment, Government Revenue, GDP
Cons:
- Firms become protected (inefficient, lazy, less innovative) leading to poor quality products


Overvalued Currency
Government deliberately increases the value of its currency
- cheaper machinerys, equipments, help industrialization 

However,
Government must continuously buy its own currency.
This requires large foreign exchange reserves.

If reserves run out:
- Currency crisis
- Financial instability



Government Assistance
Government directly supports firms
- Cash grants
- Tax breaks
- Low-interest loans
- Government investment

Reduce production costs and Encourage manufacturing

However,
Government spending increases leads to Tax revenue may fall which causes budget deficit


Summary of Inward Orientation
Government attempts to industrialise through:
- Trade protection
- Import substitution
- Currency overvaluation
- Government funding

Goal:
Develop domestic manufacturing.


Why Did Inward Orientation Fail?
Many countries (especially Latin America) experienced disappointing results
- Inefficient firms
- High production costs
- Poor product quality
- Lack of innovation
- Reduced competition
- Heavy government spending
- Currency crises
- Large budget deficits

Overall economic performance remained weak.

In conclusion:
Inward Orientation (Import Substitution Industrialisation) is a Structuralist strategy, 
where govt protect infant industries through tariffs, quotas, currency overvaluation, and financial support to develop domestic manufacturing.
Although it encourages industrialisation and employment, excessive protection often leads to inefficiency, poor-quality goods, 
budget deficits, and slow economic growth, causing many countries to later adopt Outward-Oriented (export-led) growth strategies instead.

Countries Shift Towards Outward Orientation
