
Lecture 10: Managing Ethically & Sustainably

 
========================================================
1. WHAT IS CSR?
========================================================
 
- CSR = Corporate Social Responsibility = responsibility beyond profit
- Traditionally, businesses were seen as existing only to make profit for shareholders
- CSR argues businesses also have responsibilities to:
    > Society     - contributing positively to communities and social wellbeing
    > Environment - reducing pollution, waste, and environmental harm
    > Stakeholders - considering interests of everyone affected, not just shareholders
 
 
========================================================
2. WHY CSR MATTERS
========================================================
 
- Builds trust
    > Responsible behaviour earns trust from customers, employees, and the public
 
- Improves reputation
    > Ethical behaviour attracts positive media, loyal customers, supportive investors
 
- Long-term sustainability
    > Ignoring social/environmental issues risks lawsuits, backlash, resource depletion,
      and loss of customer loyalty — CSR supports long-term survival
 
- Ethical responsibility
    > Beyond business benefits, companies have a MORAL duty to avoid harm
    > Do the right thing because it is right, not only because it is profitable
 
 
========================================================
3. CSR IN PRACTICE
========================================================
 
- Genuine CSR is NOT just donating money or sponsoring events once a year
- It must be integrated into core operations:
    > Ethical sourcing of materials
    > Responsible waste management
- CSR should be: internal, strategic, and ongoing
- It should shape everyday decision-making, not just improve image
 
CASE STUDY: Who Gives a Crap
- Toilet paper brand that builds CSR into its business model
- Donates 50% of profits to sanitation and clean water projects
- Social purpose is part of the brand identity and value proposition
- Example of CSR embedded in the business model, not an afterthought
- Website: https://uk.whogivesacrap.org/
 
 
========================================================
4. CSR DIMENSIONS — THE TRIPLE BOTTOM LINE
========================================================
 
The Triple Bottom Line expands business success beyond profit alone.
Three dimensions:
 
PEOPLE (Social)
- Social impact of the organisation
- Includes: employee wellbeing, labour practices, human rights,
  community support, customer welfare
- Example: donations and sanitation initiatives help people live healthier, safer lives
 
PLANET (Environmental)
- Environmental sustainability
- Includes: reducing pollution, conserving resources, lowering waste,
  using eco-friendly practices
 
PROFIT / SOCIETY (Economic + Community Impact)
- Companies should CREATE value that benefits society, not just extract money
- The Triple Bottom Line tells managers to evaluate success in a balanced way:
    > Financial performance
    > Social impact
    > Environmental impact
- This challenges the old idea that profit is the ONLY goal
 
 
========================================================
5. BENEFITS OF CSR
========================================================
 
- Employee engagement  : Staff feel motivated and proud when the org behaves responsibly
- Recruitment          : Talented job seekers prefer employers whose values match their own
- Brand reputation     : Responsible behaviour builds a resilient brand image
- Customer value       : Consumers prefer ethical brands — strengthens loyalty
 
 
========================================================
6. SHAREHOLDER PRIMACY vs. STAKEHOLDER RESPONSIBILITY
========================================================
 
This is the CENTRAL DEBATE in business ethics.
 
PROFIT (Shareholder Primacy)            RESPONSIBILITY (Stakeholder View)
----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------
Main job = maximise profits             | Businesses affect many groups, not just shareholders
Responsibility to shareholders first    | Companies should balance profit with ethical/social duties
Measured by share price                 |
Dominant since 1970s                    |
 
Key point: Businesses need profit to survive, but if profit becomes the ONLY
priority, harmful decisions may result.
 
PROBLEMS WITH SHAREHOLDER PRIMACY:
- Short-term focus    : Managers chase quick profits, harming long-term sustainability
- Ignores stakeholders: Employees, communities, environment neglected
- Encourages risky behaviour: Pressure to maximise returns leads to cutting corners,
  hiding problems, reducing safety standards
 
CRITICISMS:
- Excludes ethical concerns (a decision can be profitable but still morally wrong)
- Ignores environmental impact (e.g. pollution for short-term gain)
- Leads to imbalance — ethical, social, ecological responsibilities pushed aside

7. CORPORATE FAILURE CASE STUDY — BP DEEPWATER HORIZON
========================================================
 
Event: Explosion on the Deepwater Horizon oil rig — April 20, 2010
- 11 workers killed
- Massive oil spill in the Gulf of Mexico
 
Management questions raised:
- Were safety procedures ignored?
- Was there pressure to save time or money?
- Did leadership create a culture where risks were underestimated?
- Were warnings overlooked?
 
ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT:
- Marine life destruction
- Widespread pollution
- Long-term ecosystem damage (affecting ecosystems for years)
- Consequences: clean-up costs, legal action, public outrage, lasting mistrust
 
HUMAN IMPACT:
- Workers killed and injured
- Families suffered grief and financial hardship
- Communities lost income, safety, and quality of life
- When ethics fail, costs are often borne by ordinary workers, not just executives
 
FINANCIAL IMPACT:
- Job losses
- Billions in financial losses
- Economic disruption to coastal communities
- Companies ignoring CSR may face: lawsuits, compensation claims, fines,
  cleanup costs, falling investor confidence, operational disruptions
 
REPUTATIONAL DAMAGE:
- Loss of public trust
- Stock price collapse
- Long-term brand damage
- Trust influences: customer loyalty, investor confidence, employee morale,
  partnerships, regulator attitudes
- Reputational damage often outlasts the immediate crisis
 
KEY LESSON: Lack of CSR = long-term failure
Even large, profitable, globally recognised companies can suffer enormous
long-term damage from poor ethics and poor responsibility.

- Ethical responsibility  : Organisations should avoid harm and act fairly
- Business sustainability : Responsible practices help firms survive long-term
- Stakeholder trust       : Trust is essential for stable relationships with
                            employees, customers, regulators, investors, and communities

9. CARROLL'S CSR MODEL
========================================================
 
CSR has FOUR layers of responsibility (often shown as a pyramid):
 
1. ECONOMIC (Base)
   - A business must be profitable
   - Without profit, it cannot survive, pay employees, or invest in the future
 
2. LEGAL
   - A business must obey the law
   - Includes: labour law, environmental law, taxation, safety regulations
 
3. ETHICAL
   - A business should do what is right, fair, and just — even when not legally required
   - Law sets the MINIMUM standard; ethics asks for HIGHER standards
 
4. DISCRETIONARY / PHILANTHROPIC (Top)
   - Voluntary contributions to society
   - Examples: donations, community programmes, support for social causes
 
Reference: Carroll, AB 1991, 'The pyramid of corporate social responsibility',
Business Horizons, vol. 34, no. 4, pp. 39-48.

10. ETHICS IN ORGANISATIONS
========================================================
 
- Values guide behaviour
    > Values shape what is considered acceptable
    > Influence hiring, communication, performance expectations, stakeholder treatment
 
- Must align with strategy
    > If a company says it values integrity but only rewards sales results,
      employees may ignore ethics — strategy and values must align
 
- Requires leadership commitment
    > Leaders SET THE TONE
    > If leaders behave unethically, employees learn values are not real
    > Ethical culture starts from the top
 
 
========================================================
11. CODE OF ETHICS
========================================================
 
WHAT IS IT?
- A written document explaining expected standards of conduct
- May cover: honesty, conflicts of interest, workplace behaviour,
  confidentiality, anti-bribery, fairness, legal compliance
- Purpose: give employees clarity on what the organisation stands for
  and what behaviour is unacceptable
 
PURPOSE:
- Set behaviour standards   — employees need clarity on expected conduct
- Ensure compliance         — reduce misconduct, support legal compliance
- Promote stakeholder trust — confidence in dealing with the organisation
 
TYPES:
- Corporate codes     : organisation-specific; reflects internal values and rules
- Professional codes  : apply to professions (doctors, lawyers, accountants, teachers)
- Industry codes      : apply across a sector; create shared standards
 
 
========================================================
12. WHISTLEBLOWING
========================================================
 
- Reporting unethical behaviour within an organisation
- Risks: retaliation, job loss, career damage, isolation
- Protections: legal protections, company whistleblower policies
- Employees are often the FIRST to notice misconduct
- Ethical organisations should create safe reporting channels
  and protect those who speak up in good faith
 
 
========================================================
13. CORPORATE FAILURE CASE STUDY — ENRON
========================================================
 
Background:
- Enron was an energy company that appeared highly successful
- Was secretly hiding debt and overstating profits through deceptive accounting
- When fraud was exposed: company collapsed, employees lost jobs and savings
- Became a landmark case of corporate ethics failure
 
Key lesson — Enron HAD formal systems and codes, but it still failed. Why?
- Because organisational culture rewarded greed, secrecy, manipulation,
  and excessive risk-taking — the written code became meaningless
 
Lessons:
- Ethics must be PRACTISED, not just written
- Ethics cannot be reduced to paperwork
- Leaders must MODEL ethical behaviour
- Organisational incentives must SUPPORT ethical decisions
- Culture is more powerful than written rules
 
 
========================================================
14. KEY TAKEAWAYS
========================================================
 
- Ethics and sustainability go beyond profit
- CSR = responsibility to society, environment, and stakeholders
- Good CSR builds trust, reputation, and long-term success
- The Triple Bottom Line: people, planet, profit
- CSR should be part of business strategy — not an afterthought
- Shareholder primacy can create ethical and sustainability risks
- BP: shows the cost of poor ethics and poor CSR
- Enron: shows that culture matters more than written rules
- Codes of ethics only work when leaders model ethical behaviour
- Sustainable success depends on RESPONSIBLE LEADERSHIP
