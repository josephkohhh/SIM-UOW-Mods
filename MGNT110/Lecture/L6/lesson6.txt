MGNT110 — Power and Politics (Lecture 6)
What is Power?

The capacity of an individual or group to influence others and shape outcomes
Fundamental to organisations — like oxygen to breathing (Clegg et al., 2006)
The ability to make things happen, or influence what happens

What is Politics?

How power is used in practice — through techniques of influence and various tactics
Not just having power, but how people influence others, get what they want, and shape decisions behind the scenes

Tactics can be positive (collaboration, networking, persuasion) or negative (manipulation, withholding information, backstabbing)

Case Study: Challenger Shuttle (1986)

Engineers warned of a serious technical fault (O-ring failure)
Management ignored warnings and proceeded with the launch
Result: explosion and loss of lives
Key lesson: this was a power and politics failure, not just a technical one — power can distort decisions, silence voices, and lead to catastrophic consequences


The Four Faces of Power (Fleming & Spicer, 2014)
Face 1 — Coercion (Dahl, 1957)

Direct, visible power — A forces B to do something B would not otherwise do
Example: boss orders an employee to stay late

Face 2 — Manipulation / Hidden Power (Bachrach & Baratz, 1962)

Power works by controlling the agenda — deciding what gets discussed and what gets ignored
If something is never discussed, it can never be challenged

Face 3 — Domination (Stephen Lukes)

Shapes what people think and want, not just what they do
People may accept or support outcomes that aren't in their own best interest

Face 4 — Subjectification (Fleming & Spicer, 2014)

The deepest form: power shapes your identity
People internalise organisational expectations and come to see certain behaviours as normal, professional, or desirable


Foucault's Governmentality & Bentham's Panopticon
Bentham's Panopticon

A prison design where inmates could be watched at any time but couldn't tell when they actually were being observed
Result: people self-regulate continuously because surveillance might be happening
Modern workplace equivalents: KPI systems, email monitoring, attendance tracking, CCTV, performance reviews, learning analytics

Foucault's Governmentality

Power works from the inside — people govern themselves through internalised norms
Coercion = "I obey because I am forced"
Governmentality = "I obey because this is what a good employee does"
Modern organisations achieve compliance through culture, norms, and self-surveillance — without needing harsh visible control


Soft Domination & Concertive Control
Soft Domination

Power can appear polite, participative, and empowering on the surface, while still guiding behaviour and limiting autonomy
Example: no manager orders you to stay until 9pm, but team norms make leaving early feel disloyal

Concertive Control

Control comes not from a boss but from the team itself
In self-managing teams, the group internalises organisational values and disciplines itself and each other
Control is not absent — it is relocated from hierarchy to peers


Key Takeaways

Power is about influence, not just position
Politics is how power operates in practice
Power can be visible (coercion) or invisible (norms, culture, identity)
Even "empowerment" and "flat" team structures can be subtle forms of control
Understanding power helps explain how decisions are really made
