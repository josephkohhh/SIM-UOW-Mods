MGNT110 – Lecture 9: Change and Innovation


What is Change?
- Transition from one stable state to another
- In reality, organisations are never fully stable — always adjusting to market shifts, new technology, staff turnover, competitive pressure
- Good change management = designing & communicating change people can understand, not forcing acceptance

Three Types of Change
Incremental - Small, gradual improvements, Less disruptive, Feels slow, Improving customer service step by step
Punctuated Equilibrium - Long stability → sudden revolution, Explains major transformation, Highly disruptive, Apple iPhone launch
Continuous - Change never stops, Suits fast-moving environments, Can cause fatigue, Google/Meta constant updates


4 Approaches to Managing Change
1. Lewin's Model (OD)
Planned, structured — Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze

Unfreeze: create need, reduce resistance
Change: learn new behaviours
Refreeze: reinforce and embed new practices

2. Planned Approach — Kotter's 8 Steps
Create urgency → Build coalition → Develop vision → Communicate vision → Empower employees → Short-term wins → Consolidate gains → Anchor in culture
3. Processual Approach — Dawson's Framework
Change is messy, political, and non-linear. Three dimensions:

Context — internal (people, leadership) & external (market, politics)
Politics — internal conflict/resistance & external lobbying/alliances
Substance — type, scale, timing, attitudes toward change


4. Situational Approach — Palmer's Images
Managers play different roles depending on level of control and predictability of outcomes:

Director — controlling, intended outcomes
Coach — shaping, intended outcomes
Navigator — controlling, partially intended outcomes
Interpreter — shaping, partially intended outcomes
Caretaker — controlling, unintended outcomes
Nurturer — shaping, unintended outcomes


Innovation
Definition: Implementation of a new or significantly improved product, service, or organisational process — both a process and an outcome
Process: Initiation → Development → Implementation
Two views of innovation:
Innovation as Material Techniques — structured, manager-controlled, follows clear processes. Like a recipe.
Innovation as Emergence — unpredictable, context-driven, trial and error. Like growing a plant — you create conditions but cannot fully control the outcome.
