MGNT110 — Managing Conflict & Communication

What is Conflict?

Occurs when people have different goals, values, or expectations
Not always obvious — sometimes perceived, not real

Why Conflict Happens

Power differences — e.g. boss vs employee
Identity differences — culture, gender, background
Resource competition — time, money, grades

Levels of Conflict

Interpersonal — between individuals (most common)
Intergroup — between teams
Inter-organisational — between companies

Types of Conflict

Task — about the work itself
Relationship — personal/emotional
Process — about how work is done
Status — about who does what / who has authority


Functional vs Dysfunctional Conflict
Functional (Constructive)

Supports group goals, improves performance, drives creativity and problem-solving

Dysfunctional (Destructive)

Hinders performance, disrupts cohesion, creates a negative environment

Perspectives on Conflict

Traditional view — conflict = bad, avoid it
Human relations view — conflict = natural
Interactionist view — conflict = necessary (modern organisations prefer this)

Theoretical Perspectives
Dialectical Perspective (Hegel)

Conflict = two opposing ideas (thesis vs antithesis) → resolved into a better combined idea (synthesis)
Conflict is a natural process that improves ideas over time

Dialogical Perspective (Bakhtin)

No single correct answer; multiple voices exist
Organisations = many voices, not one truth
Meaning is created between people in ongoing exchanges

Music Metaphor

Organisation = orchestra; individuals = instruments; outcome = the music
Conflict is not just noise — when managed well, it creates harmony


Communication
Organisations as Communication Systems

Most conflicts stem from communication breakdown, not the task itself
Communication connects tasks, coordinates people, and engages stakeholders
Common causes of conflict: misunderstanding, assumptions, unclear messages

Active Listening

Good communication = not just talking; includes listening, empathy, and understanding
Pay attention to both content and unstated messages (tone, non-verbal cues)

Polyphony

Organisations are made up of multiple voices, each contributing to meaning
No single voice holds the whole truth
Encouraging diverse perspectives reduces groupthink and mitigates conflict


Key Takeaways

Conflict is inevitable but not always bad
Communication is the most common root cause of conflict
Multiple perspectives (polyphony) lead to better decisions and less conflict
