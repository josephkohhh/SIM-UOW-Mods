MGNT110 – Lecture 8: Managing Knowledge and Learning

Types of Knowledge

Explicit

What it is - Knowledge we consciously talk about
How it's stored - Documented, recorded
Key trait - Visible and shareable


Tacit
What it is - Embodied, intuitive, unconscious
How it's stored - Experience-based, hard to transfer
Key trait - "We know more than we can tell" (Polanyi)


Knowledge Hierarchy (Tsoukas & Vladimirou, 2002)
Data → Information → Knowledge → Practical Action


Why Learning Matters

Learning = adaptation, not memorisation
Rogers (1969): the educated person is one who has learned how to learn
Kolb (2015): humans survive by adapting reactively (fitting in) and proactively (shaping the world)



Exploitation vs Exploration (March, 1995)

Exploitation
Focus - Refining what's known
Keywords - Efficiency, repetition
Risk - Low, short-term gains

Exploration
Focus - Discovering what's new
Keywords - Innovation, risk, discovery
Risk - High, long-term potential


Netflix example:
DVD improvements = Exploitation
Shift to streaming (2007) = Exploration


Dreyfus Model — 5 Stages of Skill Acquisition
1. Novice — follows rules mechanically
2. Advanced Beginner — gains real-world context
3. Competence — overwhelmed; starts making plans
4. Proficiency — intuition begins replacing rules
5. Expertise — acts instinctively; can't always explain 

Experts must still reflect critically to refine and maintain their expertise.


Manager's Role in Learning
- Foster emotional involvement in learning
- Allow learners to voice ideas and challenge
- Share own successes and failures
- Use experts as role models for others
